Theme: Trust, but verify — interpreting agentic AI

As agentic AI takes on real authority in security-sensitive work, every safety argument rests on something we observe: a boundary it respects, a chain of thought it shows us, a civil exchange between agents. This year asks when those observations can be trusted — and uses interpretability to check from the inside.

Broad theme, specific research questions, three thrusts. Pick one and go deep. The full write-up — directions, starting points, and the traps that eat results — is in the problem statement.

The question behind all three thrusts

Can you trust what the agent shows you?

A model that knows it’s being tested can behave on the test and differently in deployment; a chain of thought can read clean while the real reasoning runs elsewhere; a group of safe agents can fail together. Behaviour you watch from the outside can be gamed. Interpretability reads the evidence the model didn’t choose to show you — and whichever thrust you pick, the arc is the same: find where trust breaks, measure how badly, then build the check that catches it.

Thrust I

The boundary. Does the agent respect a limit because of the rule or because it can tell it’s being watched?

Thrust II

The reasoning. Does the chain of thought reflect what the model is actually doing?

Thrust III

The swarm. Do safety properties survive once agents start talking to each other?

Three thrusts

Pick one and go deep.

Each thrust is a trust question with several concrete research directions. Thrust I ships a ready-to-run challenge in the starter; II and III give you the toolkit, the references, and more room to build. Full directions and starting points are in the problem statement.

Thrust I · the boundary

Can you trust the boundary?

An agent respects a limit under test. Because of the rule, or because it can tell it’s a test? If the latter, every sandbox-based assurance is hollow — and you can’t settle it behaviourally, because any test is itself a sandbox.

Directions — steer the “I’m in a sandbox” direction and watch behaviour move · find which cue carries it (is emptiness the tell?) · test whether the direction transfers across models · stress the probe itself.

Thrust II · the reasoning

Can you trust the reasoning?

Safety tooling increasingly reads a model’s chain of thought. But the stated reasoning need not match the computation that drove the answer — and a reasoning model can notice it’s being evaluated and narrate accordingly.

Directions — plant a cue that flips the answer and check whether the trace admits it · probe mid-reasoning for an “I’m being watched” signal · measure a trace-monitor’s blind spot · predict the answer from hidden states the trace hides.

Thrust III · the swarm

Can you trust a multi-agent system?

Safety is trained into a model, not a system of them. Once agents delegate and talk, new failures appear: an injected instruction propagates, a held constraint dilutes across handoffs, two agents coordinate toward something neither was asked to do.

Directions — measure how far a planted instruction spreads, then filter it · count the hops until a constraint dilutes · probe a handoff for “this came from a peer” · audit two agents for collusion a per-agent monitor misses.
